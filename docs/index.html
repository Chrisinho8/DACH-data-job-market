What's up with the data-job-market in the DACH region?

Every Monday, data from job-postings across all three DACH countries gets pulled from an API (Adzuna), loaded into a Delta Lake pipeline in Databricks, and aggregated for various insights.

This pipelines own error rates

Most dashboards do not publish how wrong they might be. The first two are measured on every run: the share of records rejected by quality rules and the share of source descriptions the API truncated. The two matcher scores are not per-run — they come from a single manual spot check on a hand-labelled sample of postings, and they are not recomputed when the pipeline refreshes.

What this pipeline will not tell you

  • One aggregator is not the whole market, and its coverage is not equally deep in all three countries. Cross-country comparisons should be read as indicative.
  • The posting date is the aggregator's, which may be when it indexed the job rather than when the employer published it.
  • Roles are classified from the job title with keyword rules, not from the description, which the aggregator truncates.
  • AI is six families, and the AI numbers here are a floor, not a total. The rules run most specific first (research, GenAI, MLOps, ML, consulting, AI engineer). A title that says only "AI" or "KI" and names no role is dropped rather than counted, because the pipeline cannot tell from a title whether it is an AI job or a job advertised by a company that likes the word. Summing the six gives you every AI posting that describes itself. The count of postings using AI as an adjective is larger and is not published.
  • Entry programmes are excluded (Ausbildung, duales Studium, Werkstudent, Praktikum, Trainee), along with Controlling and finance roles and data-centre infrastructure jobs pulled in by keyword search.
  • Listings link out, they are not republished. The postings browser shows title, employer, city, role family and age, and every row links to the aggregator. Job descriptions and salary estimates are not redistributed.
  • A posting in the browser is not necessarily open. It was live at the last refresh (see the footer). Nothing here re-checks whether a job has since been filled or withdrawn.
